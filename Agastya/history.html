🕰️ A journey through time

The story of space machines

Less than 70 years ago, nobody had ever seen a satellite. Now there are thousands. Let's travel back and watch it all happen — one giant leap at a time.

🚀 It all starts with a rocket
Once upon a time…

The beep that changed everything

On the 4th of October, 1957, a team far away launched a shiny metal ball the size of a beach ball into the sky. It was called Sputnik 1, and it did something no machine had ever done before — it went all the way around the Earth.

Radio fans all over the world tuned in and heard a soft, steady “beep… beep… beep.” That tiny sound meant one huge thing: humans could finally reach space.

📻

Sputnik was only 58 cm across and had four long antennas. It sent out radio beeps for 21 days before its batteries ran out.

The great timeline

Milestones that made history

Scroll down and watch the biggest moments come to life.

1957

Sputnik 1 🛰️

The very first satellite. A small metal sphere that beeped as it orbited the Earth every 96 minutes. The Space Age begins!

1958

Explorer 1 🔬

The United States' first satellite. It carried a science experiment that discovered invisible radiation belts around Earth — now called the Van Allen belts.

1960

TIROS-1 🌦️

The first weather satellite. It sent back the first ever TV pictures of clouds seen from space — the beginning of modern weather forecasting.

1960s

Secret eyes in the sky 👁️

Governments began launching their own reconnaissance satellites to photograph other countries from orbit. These early spy satellites started a whole new chapter — satellites used for defence and security.

1962

Telstar 1 📺

The first satellite to send live television across the ocean. Millions of people watched pictures travel through space for the very first time.

1963

Syncom 2 🛰️

The first satellite to sit still in the sky. It flew so high and so fast that it matched Earth's spin exactly — an idea called geostationary orbit. Today that's exactly where most TV, weather and big telecom satellites live.

1978

GPS is born 🧭

The first satellites of the GPS system launched. Years later they would power the maps in phones and help cars, ships and planes find their way.

1990

Hubble Space Telescope 🔭

A giant telescope placed above the clouds and air. It took breathtaking pictures of stars, planets and galaxies, and it's still working today.

1990s

Phones that work anywhere 📱

Satellite-phone networks like Iridium put a constellation of machines in the sky so a call could be made from the middle of an ocean or a desert — places with no phone towers at all.

1998

Space Station building begins 🧩

Countries around the world started joining modules together in orbit. Piece by piece, the International Space Station became the biggest satellite ever built.

2015

Rockets learn to land 🪂

Rocket boosters began flying back to Earth and landing upright, ready to fly again. Space became cheaper and much more reusable.

2019

Internet constellations 🛰️🛰️🛰️

Companies began launching thousands of small satellites working together as a team. These "constellations" — like Starlink — beam fast internet down to almost anywhere on the planet.

2021

James Webb Space Telescope 🌌

The most powerful space telescope ever built launched. It peers back in time to see the very first galaxies — with a mirror the size of a tennis court net spread out in space.

Today

12,800+ and counting 🚀

Right now, thousands of human-made satellites circle the Earth — carrying our calls and TV, guiding us, watching the weather, keeping countries safe, and helping us understand our world better than ever before.

Today — Modern satellites

Flying computers and cameras.

  • ✓Some are as big as a bus with solar wings
  • ✓Powerful cameras and ultra-clear sensors
